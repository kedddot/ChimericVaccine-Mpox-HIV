Dear Prof. Doytchinova and colleagues,

I wrote on 30 August with a short 8-sequence pilot, ahead of a larger job. I
know the servers are mid-migration and that offline jobs are being run by hand,
so I am not chasing that message - I am replacing it. This one is self-contained
and supersedes it entirely.

Please disregard the 8 sequences in that earlier message. Our candidate set was
regenerated after I sent it, and none of those 8 peptides are in the final pool,
so screening them would be wasted effort on your side. The 184 below are the
real job.

I am a senior high school student researcher at Caloocan City Science High
School, Philippines, working on an in silico multi-epitope vaccine design study
targeting HIV-1 CRF01_AE and Monkeypox virus Clade II. Our methodology specifies
AllerTOP and AllergenFP together as a two-predictor consensus, so both of your
servers are central to the work.

I saw the notice that jobs under 500 sequences are currently processed offline
free of charge during the migration. Since both tools are hosted by your group,
I hope it is acceptable to request both in a single message - please tell me if
you would rather I send them separately.

messages, please just say so and I will resend them however suits your queue.

THREE QUESTIONS, IF YOU HAVE A MOMENT

1. Roughly how long does an offline job usually take to come back? I ask only so
   I can plan around it, not to hurry you.

2. Is 184 peptides acceptable as a single file, or would you prefer it split?

3. This is the one I would most value your answer on. Our candidates are short
   peptides, 9 to 16 amino acids. In our own testing, AllergenFP appeared unable
   to return a result for sequences below 16 residues, which we assumed is
   because the ACC fingerprint cannot be computed on a peptide that short. We
   have written that into our methods as our own observation, because we could
   not find the limit stated in the AllergenFP paper.

   Could you confirm whether that is correct, and whether there is a documented
   minimum length for either tool? The same question applies to AllerTOP: 127 of
   the 184 sequences here are below 16 aa, and I would rather know now if the
   method was not intended for peptides that short.

   If either tool is not appropriate at these lengths, we would genuinely rather
   say so plainly in the manuscript than report numbers the methods were not
   designed to produce. Our study already treats the assembled construct
   (~500 aa, well within the intended range) as where the allergenicity claim
   properly rests, and reports the per-peptide screen with its limitations
   stated - so an honest "no" from you costs us nothing and improves the paper.

Both tools are cited in the manuscript (Dimitrov et al., AllerTOP v.2;
Dimitrov et al., AllergenFP). Thank you for keeping them freely available, and
for running jobs by hand during the migration.
