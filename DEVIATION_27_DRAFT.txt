27. ALLERGENICITY -- PER-PEPTIDE PREDICTION DOES NOT WORK AT EPITOPE LENGTHS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
DRAFT for Methods_Deviations_RRL_Support.txt. Compiled 2026-08-31.

CHANGED: Sec. I.E screens every candidate peptide for allergenicity using
AllerTOP v2.0 and AllergenFP v1.0. Those two predictors are retained, but a
third check is added ahead of them -- an FAO/WHO contiguous-identity screen
against a curated allergen set -- and the DEFINITIVE allergenicity call is
moved to the assembled construct at Sec. II.A, where the tools are valid.

WHY. The candidates are 9-16 aa. Every predictor available was tested at those
lengths and none can operate there:

  AllergenFP v1.0   Cannot compute its ACC fingerprint below 16 aa. This was
                    already deviation #6, recorded as our own observation. It
                    means 127 of the 184 candidates that reached this stage
                    (all MHC-I 9/10-mers and all MHC-II 15-mers) never receive
                    a second predictor at all. Only the 57 B-cell 16-mers can.

  AllerCatPro 2.0   Tested 2026-08-31 with a POSITIVE CONTROL. The same allergen
                    (Bet v 1, from AllerCatPro's own sample set) was submitted
                    whole and progressively truncated:
                        160 aa -> strong evidence (100% identity)
                         16 aa -> strong evidence (69.2%)
                         15 aa -> strong evidence (61.5%)
                         10 aa -> NO EVIDENCE
                          9 aa -> NO EVIDENCE
                    A human-derived 16-mer negative control correctly returned
                    no evidence, so the run was discriminating. The tool reports
                    "No significant hit (E-value threshold 0.001)" below ~15 aa:
                    a short query carries too little information to reach BLAST
                    significance. This is a STATISTICAL FLOOR, not a biological
                    verdict.

  AlgPred 2.0       Installable and batch-capable, so it was tested as a
                    candidate replacement. On 1,600 labelled peptides (800 cut
                    from reviewed allergens, 800 from the human proteome) it
                    scored AUC 0.611 -- barely above the 0.5 of a coin flip --
                    and at its default 0.3 threshold called 78.9% of HUMAN
                    peptides allergenic. Rejected on merit, not adopted.

CONSEQUENCE: a "non-allergen" call on a 9-mer from any of these tools means
"could not be assessed", not "safe". Reporting it as safety evidence would
repeat exactly the error deviation #1 corrected for raw ToxinPred scores, where
the tool labelled human albumin, human GAPDH and this study's own adjuvant toxic.

WHAT IS DONE INSTEAD -- three layers, each reported for what it can support:

  1. FAO/WHO CONTIGUOUS-IDENTITY SCREEN (new, automated, local, all candidates)
     Asks whether a peptide shares an identical contiguous stretch with a known
     allergen. A factual lookup, not a prediction, so it has NO minimum length.
     Reference: UniProtKB keyword KW-0020, reviewed only, 1,020 curated
     allergens, 233,403 residues (allergen_db/).
     WINDOW = 8 residues. Codex specifies >=6, but the 6-mer rule is documented
     as over-sensitive (Silvanovich et al. 2006; Hileman et al. 2002) and that
     was reproduced here: w=6 flags 6.1% of ordinary human peptides. At w=8 the
     human false-positive rate is 0.4% while positive controls are still
     detected 100% of the time at 9, 10, 15 and 16 aa.
     MEASURED ON THIS STUDY'S CANDIDATES: 0.00% of 6,000 sampled peptides show
     allergen homology at w=8, and 0 of the 184 that entered Sec. I.E
     allergenicity screening (re-measured 2026-08-31 on the rebuilt pool, with
     200/200 allergen-derived positive controls detected at 9/10/15/16 aa and
     7/2,000 human-proteome fragments flagged = 0.35%).
     THIS WORKS WHERE THE PREDICTORS DO NOT -- it detects a 9-aa Bet v 1
     fragment that AllerCatPro cannot see.

  2. AllerTOP v2.0 + AllergenFP v1.0 on the candidate pool (unchanged from
     Sec. I.E). Retained because they are the cited method. Reported with the
     length limitation stated, and with AllergenFP's coverage (57 of 184)
     stated rather than implied.

  3. THE DEFINITIVE CALL, at the assembled construct (Sec. II.A, already in the
     pipeline). The construct is ~500 aa -- the size range these tools were
     built and validated for -- and receives full AllerTOP + AllergenFP
     consensus. This is where the allergenicity claim properly rests.

BIOLOGICAL BASIS FOR MOVING THE WEIGHT TO THE CONSTRUCT: an allergic response
requires IgE cross-linking, which needs a molecule large enough to present two
epitopes simultaneously (Aalberse 2000, J Allergy Clin Immunol). A free 9-mer
generally cannot do this. Asking whether an isolated 9-mer "is an allergen" is
therefore not a well-posed question, which is the underlying reason all three
tools fail at that length rather than a coincidence of implementation.

OURS, NOT FROM THE LITERATURE -- STATE AS SUCH:
  - The AllerCatPro length floor (our controlled Bet v 1 experiment)
  - The AlgPred AUC 0.611 measurement
  - The choice of an 8-residue window for this study
  - The 0.00% homology result on this study's candidates
  - The FAO/WHO criteria were written for GM crop proteins; applying them to
    vaccine epitopes is a considered choice, not established practice

HONEST SUMMARY: this does not make the per-peptide allergenicity screen strong.
It makes it honest. The screen now reports allergen HOMOLOGY, which it can
measure at any length, instead of allergenicity PREDICTION, which no available
tool can perform on a 9-mer. The safety claim rests on the construct-level
assessment, where the methods are valid.
